Chapter 5

A Sunny Morning

Serafín and Joaquín Álvarez Quintero
One-act play (comedy)
⚡ Tip Sheet 📝 Revision Notes 🎯 Themes & Analysis

⚡ Quick Tip Sheet

👤 Authors

  • Serafín (1871–1938) and Joaquín (1873–1944) Álvarez Quintero
  • Spanish brothers, known as the "Golden Boys of Madrid Theatre"
  • Celebrated playwrights of the early 20th century
  • Wrote about 200 plays together
  • Their plays show the life, manners and speech of the people of Andalusia (Southern Spain)
  • Their comedies helped revive Spanish theatre
  • Plays: The Flowers, The Merry Heart, One Hundred Years Ago, The Galley Slave, Apple of His Eye

🎭 Characters

Dona Lauraabout 70, white-haired, refined, mind still sharp
PetraLaura's maid, 20, in love with the park guard

🏞️ Setting

  • A sunny morning in a quiet ("retired") corner of a park in Madrid
  • Season: Autumn (a hint at old age)
  • A bench at the right
  • Laura's parasol doubles as a cane

🌹 The Past Romance

  • Villa Maricela, near Valencia, by the sea, among lemon and orange trees
  • Laura's full name: Laura Llorente, known as "The Silver Maiden"
  • Her window had red roses
  • Gonzalo rode by on horseback every morning and tossed up a bouquet. She tossed one back in the afternoon.
  • Her family wanted her to marry a merchant
  • Duel at sunrise on the beach: the merchant was badly wounded and Gonzalo had to flee

📝 Revision Notes

1. Laura in the park

Laura arrives with Petra, glad her seat is free. She sends Petra off to her guard (for no more than 10 minutes) and feeds the pigeons, which she knows one by one. She is a regular visitor: "They know just when to expect me."

2. The quarrel

  • Gonzalo arrives grumbling. Three priests are on "his" bench, so he walks through the birds and scares them away.
  • Laura scolds him. He replies, "This is a public park." She points out that he complains about the priests taking his bench.
  • With no other seat, he sits at the far end of her bench. They trade insults: he calls her a "senile old lady", she mocks his handkerchief, his glasses and his hunting.

3. Peace through snuff

Gonzalo offers her snuff. Both sneeze three times, and "the snuff has made peace between us." He reads Campoamor's verses aloud. Laura reads "Twenty years pass…" without glasses, but only because she knows it by heart.

4. Recognition

  • Gonzalo says he is from Valencia. Laura mentions the villa Maricela, and he is startled.
  • He names Laura Llorente, and she is startled too. She claims Laura was her "best friend".
  • As they piece the story together (bouquets, merchant, duel), each secretly realises who the other is ("Gonzalo!" / "This is Laura!").

5. The fictitious stories

Gonzalo pretends the lover was his "cousin" who died gloriously in Africa. Laura pretends her "friend" was swept out to sea. Both know the other is lying. The truth: she married two years later, and he ran off with a ballet dancer in three months.

6. The ending

  • They agree to meet tomorrow, if it is sunny, on the same bench. He promises to bring crumbs.
  • Each decides not to reveal themselves, because they are old and changed, and would rather be remembered as their younger selves.
  • Laura drops some violets. Gonzalo painfully stoops to pick them up, and she sees him: "Yes, it is he!" He thinks, "It is she, and no mistake."
  • They part quoting the poem: "Can it be that this is he?" / "Heavens, is it she?"

Quick answers

  • Why annoyed at first? He scared her birds. She felt he had rudely taken her bench, and he found her interfering.
  • Why Gonzalo fled Valencia: he wounded the merchant (a respected man) in a duel over Laura.
  • Why the made-up stories? To hide their identities and feelings, and to protect their romantic image of the past.
  • Who is more passionate? Neither stayed faithful (she married, he ran off), but both still hold on to the memory tenderly.
  • Is Laura an effective narrator? Yes. Her story of the sunset, the name in the sand and the rising tide is vivid and dramatic.

🎯 Themes & Analysis

1. Love & memory

Their youthful romance lives on in memory. Both choose to keep the beautiful illusion of the past rather than face the plain truth of the present.

2. Old age vs youth

Gout, canes, huge glasses and the "grotesque" look of age are contrasted with the black-eyed girl and the gallant horseman. Petra and the guard mirror the young Laura and Gonzalo.

3. Dramatic irony

The audience knows, through the asides, what the characters hide. Each lies to the other while knowing the other is lying, and Campoamor's lines ("Twenty years pass…") predict their own story.

4. Pride & vanity

Both invent heroic, tragic deaths for themselves ("decorating my own funeral") to look faithful and romantic, and to hide the less flattering truth.

5. Fate & chance

"The ways of Providence are strange." A chance meeting over a bench turns strangers into old friends again.

6. Companionship

The play moves from hostility to warmth. Two lonely old people find pleasure in each other's company and promise to meet again.

Title: "A Sunny Morning"

The setting is literally a sunny morning, and they will meet again "if it is sunny". The sunshine also stands for the warmth that melts their quarrel and the brightness of the old love it brings back. This contrasts with autumn, which stands for old age.

Tone & style: light, witty and gently sad. It is a one-act comedy built on quick-fire dialogue, asides and irony.
