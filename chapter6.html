Chapter 6

When You Are Old

W.B. Yeats
Poem (love lyric)
⚡ Tip Sheet 📝 Revision Notes 🎯 Themes & Analysis

⚡ Quick Tip Sheet

👤 Author

  • William Butler Yeats: 1865–1939
  • Irish poet, one of the foremost figures of 20th-century literature
  • Early poetry: part of the Celtic Twilight / Irish Literary Revival, using Ireland's history, myths and heroic figures
  • Also wrote plays. A founder of the Abbey Theatre, Dublin
  • Witnessed the revolutionary politics of Ireland
  • Nobel Prize for Literature: 1923
  • Later wrote modernist poetry under the influence of Ezra Pound
  • One of the great love poets of the world

🗣️ Context

  • Speaker: the lover
  • Addressed to: his beloved, who is young now
  • He imagines her in the future, old and reading the book of poems he is writing now
  • Extra (not in the textbook): the poem was written for Maud Gonne, the woman Yeats loved, and is based on a sonnet by the French poet Ronsard

  • Imagery: fire, glowing bars, mountains, stars
  • Contrast: many vs one, beauty vs soul, youth vs age

✅ MCQ Answers

  • The speaker is addressing: a young woman he has loved
  • 'Book' in line two refers to: book of poems
  • 'Glad grace' suggests: her physical beauty
  • 'Pilgrim soul' means the soul: which is in quest of true love
  • 'Your changing face' suggests: the changes of age and the joys and sorrows of her life
  • 'Love fled' connotes: the loss endured by her

📝 Revision Notes

Stanza 1: The future scene

  • The lover imagines his beloved when she is old, gray-haired and sleepy, dozing by the fire.
  • He asks her to take down this book (his poems) and read it slowly.
  • As she reads, she will dream of the soft look her eyes once had and their deep shadows, that is, her youthful beauty.

Stanza 2: The many and the one

  • Many men loved her joyful, graceful moments and her beauty. Some of that love was true and some false.
  • But one man (the speaker) loved her "pilgrim soul": her restless inner search for something meaningful.
  • He also loved the sorrows of her changing face. His love would last as she aged and suffered, not just while she was beautiful.

Stanza 3: Love fled

  • Bending beside the glowing fire, she will murmur a little sadly about how Love went away.
  • Love is personified: he fled, paced on the mountains overhead and hid his face among the stars.
  • She will regret losing the one true love she was offered, because it is now out of reach.

Quick answers

  • How is the 'one man' different? Others loved her outward beauty and grace, and their love was sometimes false. He loved her soul and her sorrows, which is deeper and lasting love.
  • Transient beauty vs permanent love? Yes. Beauty fades ("old and gray"), but the speaker's love for her soul does not depend on beauty. Only true love survives her ageing.
  • Time frame: the speaker writes in the present and imagines the future (her old age). In that future she recalls the past (her youth and his love). Moving between the three shows how time destroys beauty but not true love, and warns her about future regret.
  • Purpose of contrasts: youth vs age, many vs one, false vs true, beauty vs soul, glowing fire vs love that fled. They make the speaker's love stand out as rare and sincere, and highlight her loss.

🎯 Themes & Analysis

1. True love vs infatuation

The many admirers were attracted by beauty, which is infatuation. The one man loved her soul, which is true love. This answers the pre-reading question about the difference between the two.

2. Transience of beauty

Youthful beauty (the "soft look", "glad grace") fades with time into gray hair and sleepiness. Beauty is temporary.

3. Permanence of true love

Love for the "pilgrim soul" and "sorrows" does not fade, because it was never based on looks.

4. Regret & loss

The poem is a gentle warning. One day she will sadly realise she let the one true love slip away. Love has "fled" beyond her reach, to the mountains and stars.

5. Memory & the power of poetry

"This book" keeps the past alive. The poem itself becomes the lasting record of his love.

Symbols

  • Fire / glowing bars: warmth, memory and the fading glow of life.
  • Book: the poet's love, preserved in words.
  • Mountains & stars: love that is lofty, distant, unreachable and eternal.

Tone & style: tender, gentle and melancholic. It is a short, musical lyric with a regular ABBA rhyme, and it ends on a note of quiet sadness.
