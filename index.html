Chapter 1

Romeo and Juliet

William Shakespeare
Poem: two extracts from the play
⚡ Tip Sheet 📝 Revision Notes 🎯 Themes & Analysis

⚡ Quick Tip Sheet

👤 Author

  • William Shakespeare: 1564–1616
  • English poet & playwright
  • Called the "Bard of Avon" and England's national poet
  • The world's greatest (pre-eminent) dramatist
  • His plays are translated into every major language and performed more than any other playwright's

🏰 Background

  • Place: Verona
  • Capulets (Juliet) vs Montagues (Romeo): sworn enemies
  • Juliet is the daughter of Lord Capulet
  • Occasion: Lord Capulet's grand supper
  • Romeo goes in disguise to see Roseline, but sees Juliet on the dance floor

🎭 The Two Extracts

RomeoAct I, Scene V (first sight, at the supper)
JulietAct III, Scene II (waiting for night and Romeo)

💬 Romeo's Key Phrases

  • "teach the torches to burn bright"
  • "hangs upon the cheek of night"
  • "a rich jewel in an Ethiope's ear"
  • "Beauty too rich for use, for earth too dear"
  • "a snowy dove trooping with crows"
  • "I ne'er saw true beauty till this night"

💬 Juliet's Key Phrases

  • "Come night, come Romeo"
  • "thou day in night"
  • "Whiter than new snow on a raven's back"
  • "loving, black-browed night"
  • "cut him out in little stars"
  • "pay no worship to the garish sun"

✍️ Figures of Speech

  • Similes: jewel in an Ethiope's ear; snowy dove among crows; whiter than new snow on a raven's back
  • Hyperbole: she teaches the torches to burn bright
  • Personification: night (cheek of night, gentle, loving, black-browed night)
  • Metaphor: Romeo is "day in night"
  • Contrast: light and white vs dark and black

📝 Revision Notes

Romeo's speech (Act I, Scene V)

  • Juliet shines so brightly that she seems to teach the torches how to burn.
  • Against the dark night, she stands out like a rich jewel in an Ethiope's ear.
  • Her beauty is too rich to be used and too precious for this earth.
  • Among the other ladies she looks like a white dove among crows.
  • When the dance ends ("measure done"), he will go to where she stands and touch her hand, which will bless his rough ("rude") hand.
  • He asks whether he ever truly loved before (he had come for Roseline), and tells his eyes to deny it: he never saw true beauty till tonight.

Juliet's speech (Act III, Scene II)

  • She eagerly calls on night to come, because night brings Romeo.
  • Romeo is "day in night", a brightness in the darkness.
  • He will lie on the wings of night, whiter than new snow on a raven's back.
  • She asks night to give her Romeo, and when she dies, to cut him out into little stars.
  • He would make the "face of heaven" (the night sky) so beautiful that the whole world would fall in love with night and stop worshipping the gaudy ("garish") sun.

Quick answers

  • Similes Romeo uses: a rich jewel in an Ethiope's ear; a snowy dove trooping with crows.
  • How Romeo is immortalised: he is cut into little stars that light up the heavens forever.
  • "Face of heaven": the night sky, made lovelier by Romeo's stars.

🎯 Themes & Analysis

1. Love at first sight

Romeo falls for Juliet instantly and forgets Roseline. The love is sudden, total and overwhelming.

2. Light vs darkness (contrasting imagery)

Both lovers see each other as light shining in darkness: torch/night, jewel/dark skin, dove/crows, snow/raven, day/night. This contrast makes the beloved stand out as unique. It also hints that their love shines against the dark background of the family feud.

3. Beauty as divine

"For earth too dear": Juliet's beauty is heavenly and too precious for the ordinary world.

4. Love, death & immortality

Juliet's "when I shall die" foreshadows the play's tragic end. Her wish to turn Romeo into stars shows a love that outlives death.

5. Romeo vs Juliet: whose love is more intense?

  • Romeo: admires outward, visual beauty, and he has only just forgotten Roseline, so his love can seem hasty.
  • Juliet: her love is impatient, deep and imaginative. She wants the whole world to adore Romeo and give up the sun for him. Many readers find her love more passionate and intense.
