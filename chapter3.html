Chapter 3

On Children

Kahlil Gibran
Poem (prose poem from 'The Prophet')
⚡ Tip Sheet 📝 Revision Notes 🎯 Themes & Analysis

⚡ Quick Tip Sheet

👤 Author

  • Kahlil Gibran: 1883–1931
  • Lebanese-American artist & poet
  • His poems are called "poetic essays" because they don't follow the usual versification
  • Best-known work: 'The Prophet', translated into 25+ languages, including Kannada
  • Kannada translation in the textbook: Dr. Prabhushankara

🗣️ Context

  • A woman holding a babe against her bosom asks: "Speak to us of Children."
  • "He" = the Prophet
  • The poem critiques parents' usual expectations and urges them to introspect

✅ Parents CAN / ❌ CANNOT

❌Make them like you

💬 Key Lines

  • "Your children are not your children."
  • "sons and daughters of Life's longing for itself"
  • "They come through you but not from you"
  • "their souls dwell in the house of tomorrow"
  • "life goes not backward nor tarries with yesterday"
  • "Let your bending in the archer's hand be for gladness"
  • "He loves the arrow that flies… the bow that is stable"

📖 Glossary

House (v)shelter, protect
Tarry (v)linger, stay at one place
Archerone who uses bow & arrow (here, God)

🔍 Contrasts to Remember

  • through you ↔ not from you
  • with you ↔ not belonging to you
  • bodies ↔ souls
  • tomorrow ↔ yesterday
  • arrow that flies ↔ bow that is stable

📝 Revision Notes

  • Children are not possessions. They are born of Life's own longing to continue itself. Parents are only the channel ("through you, not from you").
  • Love, not control. Parents may give love but not their thoughts, because children have their own thoughts.
  • Bodies, not souls. Parents can shelter the body, but the child's soul lives in the "house of tomorrow" (the future), which parents cannot visit even in dreams.
  • Learn from them. Parents may try to be like their children, but must not force children to be like them, because life moves forward and never stays with yesterday.
  • Bow & arrow. Parents are bows, children are living arrows, and God is the Archer who aims at the mark on the path of the infinite. He bends the bow with His might so the arrows go swift and far.
  • Joyful surrender. Parents should accept this bending gladly. God loves the flying arrow (the child) and equally loves the stable bow (the parent).

Quick answers

  • Why "Your children are not your children"? They belong to Life/God and the future, not to their parents.
  • Bows & arrows signify: parents launch and support. Children fly ahead on their own path, guided by God.
  • Ideal attitude: loving, non-possessive, stable, and happy to sacrifice for the child's growth.

🎯 Themes & Analysis

1. Individuality & freedom

Every child has their own thoughts, soul and future. Freedom is needed in thinking, choices, beliefs and life path, with parents as steady guides.

2. Non-possessive love

True parental love means shelter and love without ownership or control.

3. Generation gap & progress

"Life goes not backward": each generation moves forward. The old should adapt to the new, not force the new into the old mould.

4. Parental responsibility

The poem is also about parents. They must be the stable bow: strong, steady, and willing to bend (sacrifice) with gladness.

5. Spiritual vision

God is the Archer. Parents and children are both part of a larger divine plan, and both are equally loved.

6. Wider readings

"Parents" can stand for the older generation, leaders, religious heads or teachers. Anyone with authority over the young should guide, not dominate.

Style: free-flowing prose poem, with a calm and wise tone, built on contrasts and an extended bow-and-arrow metaphor.
