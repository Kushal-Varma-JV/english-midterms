Chapter 2

Too Dear!

Leo Tolstoy
Short story (satire)
⚡ Tip Sheet 📝 Revision Notes 🎯 Themes & Analysis

⚡ Quick Tip Sheet

👤 Author

  • Count Leo Tolstoy: 1828–1910, Russian
  • Master of realistic fiction
  • War and Peace (1869), Anna Karenina (1877)
  • Sevastopol Sketches (1855): Crimean War
  • Trilogy: Childhood, Boyhood, Youth (1855–1858)
  • Novellas: The Death of Ivan Ilych, Family Happiness, Hadji Murad
  • Moral crisis in the 1870s made him a moral thinker and social reformer

📜 Source

  • Adapted from Guy de Maupassant's 'Sur l'eau' (French), 'Afloat' in English
  • Translated from Russian by Louise Maud & Aylmer Maud

🏝️ Monaco Facts

  • Near the borders of France & Italy, on the Mediterranean
  • Population: about 7,000
  • Not even an acre of land per person
  • Army: only 60 men
  • Taxes on: tobacco, wine & spirits, poll-tax
  • Special revenue: gaming house (roulette), the only one left in Europe
  • German rulers were forbidden to keep gaming houses because of the harm they did (ruin, suicide)

📝 Revision Notes

The "toy kingdom"

Monaco is tiny (about 7,000 people, 60 soldiers), yet it copies a real kingdom: palace, courtiers, ministers, bishop, generals, coronation, levees, courts. The Prince's main income is the gaming house. He knows it is a "dirty business" but says he has to live.

Story flow

  1. A murder happens, the first ever. The court sentences the criminal to have his head cut off, and the Prince confirms it.
  2. Problem: Monaco has no guillotine and no executioner.
  3. France quotes 16,000 francs, which is too much.
  4. Italy quotes 12,000 francs, still too much.
  5. The soldiers refuse: they were never taught how.
  6. After a Commission, a Committee and a Sub-Committee, the sentence becomes life imprisonment. It looks merciful and is cheaper.
  7. There is no proper prison, so he is kept in a makeshift place with a guard who fetches his food from the palace kitchen.
  8. After a year the cost is over 600 francs/year, and he is young and could live 50 years.
  9. The guard is dismissed, hoping he will escape. Instead he fetches his own food, returns and shuts himself in.
  10. The Minister of Justice tells him to leave. He refuses.
  11. He is offered a pension of 600 francs a year, and he agrees as long as it is paid regularly.
  12. He moves just across the frontier, buys land, starts market-gardening, and gambles 2–3 francs of his pension at the tables. He lives peaceably and well.

Why the criminal refused to leave

  • He had nowhere to go.
  • The sentence ruined his character, so people would turn their backs on him.
  • He had got out of the habit of working.
  • He felt treated unfairly: first not executed, then his guard removed, and now told to go.

🎯 Themes & Analysis

1. Satire on royalty & bureaucracy

A "toy" Prince copies the ceremony of great kings. His Councils, Commissions and Committees make a comedy of government, where every decision depends on cost.

2. Money over justice

The Prince keeps changing his decision (execution, then prison, then pension) for one reason only: each option was "too dear". Justice is decided by the budget, not by principle.

3. Hypocrisy

"You can't earn stone palaces by honest labour." The Prince lives off gambling, which ruins people, while acting as a dignified ruler of law and justice.

4. Irony

  • The punishment ends up as a reward: a pension for life.
  • The criminal refuses freedom, and the state begs him to leave.
  • His pension goes back to the Prince's gaming tables.
  • The last line praises a country that is too stingy to kill or jail him.

5. Serious questions behind the comedy

  • Should cost decide punishment?
  • Is capital punishment right?
  • What is the state's duty to a prisoner whose life and reputation it has ruined?
  • Reform vs punishment: given a chance, the "murderer" becomes a peaceful, hard-working gardener.

Tone: humorous, ironic, satirical.
